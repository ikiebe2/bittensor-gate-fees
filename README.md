## What you're actually buying

Bittensor is a decentralized network where the work is machine learning. Subnets compete to produce useful AI output — inference, training, data — and the network pays out TAO emissions to whoever contributes. Miners supply models or compute, validators score them, and stakers back the validators they think are doing honest work.

Since the dTAO upgrade, each subnet has its own token, and emissions flow toward subnets where more value is staked. That changed the economics in a way worth understanding before you buy, because it means TAO's price is partly a bet on which subnets attract capital, not just on the network's headline usage.

The numbers that matter:

- **Max supply: 21,000,000 TAO.** Circulating supply sits around 11.5 million, roughly 55% of the cap. Emissions continue, so the remaining supply arrives over time.
- **Market cap has been hovering near $3 billion**, with daily volume in the hundreds of millions of dollars.
- **The 52-week range has run from roughly $140 up to about $540.** TAO's all-time high came in April 2024, when several data providers put it above $760.

For context on the volatility: that's a coin that can halve and double again inside a year. Position sizing matters more here than on a large-cap.

## The risks worth knowing before you click buy

Published research on Bittensor flags several things that stay true regardless of which exchange you use.

**Real usage versus incentivized usage.** The open question is whether subnet activity reflects genuine AI demand or mostly token rewards chasing emissions. Inference subnets have shown real traffic, but they're a minority of total subnet activity.

**Emission gaming.** The Subnet 281 "LOL-subnet" episode showed what happens when a subnet token attracts capital without meaningful AI work behind it. dTAO improved the incentives; it didn't make the problem disappear.

**Validator concentration.** Large root validators still carry real influence over emissions. dTAO diluted that power somewhat, but governance remains concentrated.

**Dilution.** Just over half the maximum supply is circulating. Even after the first halving, emissions stay material.

**Competition and sentiment.** Bittensor leads the decentralized AI category, but Render, Akash, the ASI Alliance and Gensyn all compete for the same narrative. TAO tends to move hard on AI-sector sentiment in both directions.

None of this is a reason to avoid the asset. It's a reason to buy it with money you've already decided you can watch drop 40%.

## Three decisions that set your total cost

Before touching a buy button, three choices determine what you actually pay.

**1. How you fund the account.** Card, bank transfer, P2P, or crypto you already hold. The spread between these routes is enormous — roughly 1–5% on a card versus near zero on a bank transfer or a USDT deposit.

**2. Your region and verification status.** Gate requires identity verification to trade and withdraw. Payment options are filtered by region, and some products are restricted outright — TAO staking, for example, isn't available to users in the UK or other restricted jurisdictions.

**3. Order type and fee tier.** A market order pays taker fees and eats the spread. A limit order can pay less and sometimes gets you a better fill — if you're patient enough to wait.

## Minimums, limits and the boring things that block orders

The friction usually isn't the trade — it's everything around it.

Card purchases have minimums set by the payment provider, not by Gate, and they vary by country and currency. You'll see the floor before you confirm. P2P orders have price ranges set by each seller, so if your amount falls outside a seller's range, the offer simply won't be available to you. Bank withdrawals may take a day or two even after the transfer shows as sent.

On the crypto side, Gate also runs a TAO/USDT perpetual contract with 1–50x leverage in both directions, funded every four hours with a ±2% funding-rate cap. If you're buying spot TAO to hold, that market is irrelevant to you. If you're tempted by it, note that leverage on a coin with TAO's volatility is how accounts get liquidated on a normal Tuesday.

Withdrawals are where people make expensive mistakes. TAO moves on the Bittensor network, and Bittensor addresses look different from Ethereum addresses — long strings that typically begin with a "5". Sending TAO to an address on the wrong network is not recoverable, and no support ticket fixes it. Copy from your wallet, paste carefully, send a small test amount first if the sum is meaningful.
